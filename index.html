The 1st-of-the-month problem

Today's system is a diary, WhatsApp, and memory. All three leak.

The paper diary

Ruled register, one line per customer, a stroke a day. Get one skip-day wrong and the whole column is off. Ink smudges; the book gets wet in the monsoon.

The WhatsApp trail

"Aaj do packet." "Kal band rakhna." Thirty chats, no running total. Come billing day you scroll through a month of messages to reconstruct what was actually delivered.

The memory tax

The rest lives in your head across 80 houses. When a customer says "I only took 20 days, not 26," you have nothing to show. You round down, you eat the loss.

Multiply small errors across every flat and every day: a vendor doing 80 customers loses Rs 3,000–6,000 a month to under-billing and disputes they can't win.

The wedge

A tiffin plan is one fixed number a month. This isn't that. The quantity moves every single day — and skip-days break the sum. So we keep a per-customer daily ledger, and let the month add itself up.

Get the daily count right, and the monthly bill is no longer an argument. It's arithmetic.

How it works

Set the default once. Adjust the exceptions. The bill writes itself.

  1. 01

    Set each customer's default

    Sharma ji takes 1 litre of full-cream, every day, at Rs 62. Add it once — that's the default_qty DoodhBook assumes for tomorrow and every morning after.

  2. 02

    Tap the exceptions on your route

    Two packets today? Tap +1. Family's out of town till Monday? Mark skip. Holiday for the whole line? One tap. Everything else stays at the default.

  3. 03

    The month tallies live

    Every delivery is qty × rate, summed per product, per customer, as you go. Month-to-date value is always current — no reconstruction on the 1st.

  4. 04

    Send the bill on WhatsApp

    An itemised bill — days delivered, days skipped, total — lands in your outbox ready to send. The customer sees the same numbers you do. Dispute over.

Questions

Isn't this just a tiffin or subscription app?

No. Subscription apps assume a fixed monthly quantity. DoodhBook is built for the opposite: the count changes every day and people skip days. That daily variation is the whole point — it's what makes month-end tallying hard, and what we solve.

I'm not good with phones. Is it complicated?

If you can send a WhatsApp, you can run DoodhBook. Set a customer's default once, then it's just taps on your route — plus one, or skip. The bill builds itself; you never do the maths.

What if there's no signal in the building?

The round works offline. Mark deliveries in the basement or the lift lobby with no network, and it syncs the moment you're back on data. Nothing gets lost.
